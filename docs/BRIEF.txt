1. Vision
Crimson Desert Seamless Co-op+ turns the single-player base game (PC/Steam) into a
co-op adventure that feels like Pearl Abyss shipped it:

    Join a friend with one click through Steam; no IPs or port forwarding.
    Share story and world progression; every player can pick up loot.
    Boss and enemy health and damage scale to party size.
    Steam usernames over players' heads; friends visible on the map.
    Character customization that syncs between players.
    A quality-of-life "Comfort Pack": bigger inventories, better gliding/flying, shorter
    cooldowns, and more, with toggles.

Scope: base game only. The expansion is out of scope, and mods that aren't updated for it
are fine. The mod should be modular so it stays easy to maintain if an official multiplayer
update arrives later.

Mindset: optimistic and persistent. Ship a small working thing early, then keep improving it.

4. Research: thorough, and skeptical of "impossible"
Research how to build this before writing game code. Go deep, and save notes as you go.

Sources to mine:

   The r/CrimsonDesert co-op prototype by u/No-Context2224 (movement, jumping,
   gliding, climbing, attacks and skills synced; separate quests/loot/inventory; shared
   damage experiments; enemy sync unsolved). Read the post, comments, and any linked
   repos, videos, or Discord. If the author is reachable, consider asking to collaborate, with
   credit.
   News coverage (Insider Gaming, Notebookcheck, Tech4Gamers, Vida Extra) and Pearl
   Abyss statements about exploring official co-op or mod support.
   Nexus Mods pages, comments, bug reports, and changelogs; Steam discussions;
   Discord/modding communities; GitHub projects for Crimson Desert (tools, mod
   managers like the JSON Mod Manager, file format and asset documentation).
   Other games' co-op mods for proven patterns: Skyrim Together, Elden Ring Seamless
   Co-op, Dark Souls mods, and others (lobby flow, host-authoritative sync, scaling, save
   handling, what players loved or hated).
   Black Desert Online systems that Crimson Desert reuses or resembles.
   Steamworks documentation (matchmaking, networking sockets, rich presence, invites).
   Game-engine and reverse engineering resources relevant to Crimson Desert's engine
   (Pearl Abyss's engine and file formats), plus hooking, signature scanning, and memory
   tooling.

Do not accept "it's impossible" from the internet at face value. Forum posts, comments,
and articles are leads, not proof. For every claim that something can't be done (enemy sync,
save sharing, injecting a DLL, UI hooks, etc.):

1. Record it in NOTES/CLAIMS.md : the claim, source, date, and the reasoning given.
2. Look for the mechanism: what would have to be true for it to fail? Is the claim about this
   game, a different game, an old version, or someone's limited skills or tools?
3. Design the cheapest experiment that could prove or disprove it, and run it (in Phase B if
   it needs the game).
4. Mark it VERIFIED POSSIBLE, VERIFIED IMPOSSIBLE (with the evidence and what you
   tried), or UNTESTED. Only treat something as truly blocked after you have tested it and
   written down the evidence, and even then look for a workaround or a smaller version of
   the feature.


5. Mods, credit, and permissions
Mod authors set permissions on their Nexus pages. Treat each page as the source of truth.

   Remix/modify allowed with credit: you may adapt or include their files, following the
   page's terms, and credit the author (name, link, mod name) in the README, the Nexus
   description, and a packaged CREDITS.md .
   Allowed as-is only: do not repackage it; list it as a Requirement/Recommended mod
   with a link, and make Co-op+ compatible.
   Unclear or "ask first": use the Nexus comments/contact to ask, then skip it for now
   and keep going.
   Track everything in docs/mod-permissions.md : mod, author, link, version, permission
   summary, what we used, how we credited.
   Never strip credits or re-upload someone else's work as ours.

Nexus survey (Phase A, from your own Chrome tab): survey Crimson Desert mods by
most downloaded, most endorsed, and newest. Starting points to verify:

   Character Creator (Female/Male) by Khione95; Barber Unlocked by Aerophus; outfit
   loadout mods.
   Inventory Expander, Fat Stacks (stack sizes); Free Gliding and other better gliding/flying

   Reduced cooldowns, faster skill recharge, ability and combat tweaks, quicker fast travel,

For each: note what it does, how it is implemented (data edit, DLL, JSON, etc.), whether it

6. Feature requirements
6.1 Steam join and invites: Steam lobbies; join via overlay, friends list "Join Game," or invite
link. In-game Co-op menu (hotkey and pause menu): Host, Invite, Party list, Leave, Settings.
Rich presence, version checks with friendly messages, reconnect, host migration where
feasible. Steamworks only (matchmaking and networking sockets).

6.2 Presence: Fully animated remote players (run, jump, glide, climb, ledge pull-up,
combat, skills, effects). Steam usernames above heads (distance-faded, occluded
properly, toggleable). Friends on map and minimap with names; pings and waypoints.
Optional emotes and a party HUD.

6.3 Shared progression: Shared by default: story/quest progress, region discovery, map
unlocks, key world events. Personal by default: inventory, equipment, skills, appearance. All
in config/progression.toml . Always back up saves; never overwrite a guest's single-player
save without clear consent.

6.4 Loot: Everyone in the session can pick up world loot, chest contents, and drops. Default
is per-player instanced loot so nobody misses out; configurable alternatives (shared pile,
round-robin, need/greed). Currency and quest items go to every nearby player.

6.5 Scaling: Enemy and boss health and damage scale with party size via
config/scaling.toml (starting curve to tune, for example health x1.0 / 1.7 / 2.3 / 2.8 for 1-4
players). Boss phase and stagger thresholds scale with health. Host-authoritative. Debug
overlay for multipliers. Unit-test the math in pure sim/ . Account for QoL changes
(cooldowns, stamina) when tuning.

6.6 Customization: Integrate or recommend character creator mods; sync each player's
look to friends. Fall back to the default look if a friend lacks the mod, without crashing.

6.7 Polish: Match the game's UI style, fonts, and sounds. Clear messages, a settings
screen, and a one-click "vanilla mode."


7. Architecture and milestones
C++20, CMake, x64 DLL via an ASI/DLL loader (choose and verify a loader that works with
the game). Layers: game/ (all hooks and offsets), net/ (Steam, protocol), sim/ (scaling,
progression, loot rules; pure logic), ui/ (nameplates, map, menu), compat/ (mod
detection, version checks). Host-authoritative state, versioned binary protocol, ~10-20 Hz
snapshots with interpolation, reliable channel for events. If any hook fails, the mod disables
itself and the game runs normally. Use signature scanning rather than fixed addresses and
record findings in docs/offsets.md .
Milestones (verify each in a real two-player session during Phase B):

1. M0 Recon: loader works; probe logs player position, health, world time.
2. M1 Ghosts: Steam lobby and invites; two players see each other as markers.
3. M2 Avatars: animation sync, nameplates, map markers.
4. M3 Combat: shared enemies, damage sync, scaling for regular enemies.
5. M4 Bosses and loot: boss scaling, per-player loot.
6. M5 Progression: shared quest/discovery state with save safety.
7. M6 Comfort Pack: QoL integrated, toggles, compat tests.
8. M7 Release: packaging, credits, README, Nexus page, changelog.

Each milestone ends with a test checklist in docs/tests/ , a note in NOTES/STATE.md , and a
git commit.


8. Anti-cheat and integrity
Early on (Phase A research, then confirmed in Phase B), find out whether the game runs any
anti-cheat or integrity protection. Many mods already load for players, so it may be a non-
issue. If a protection layer does block the mod, report exactly what it is and what it checks,
and stop there. Do not disable, patch, or bypass it, since that would put every co-op friend's
account at risk. Use the officially supported launch path (normal Steam launch plus the mod
loader).

Requirements (companion mods), credits, install steps, known issues, and a backup-your-
